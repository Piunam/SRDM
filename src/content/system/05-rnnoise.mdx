### Architecture

The network follows the RNNoise design: instead of predicting a clean spectrum sample by sample, it
predicts **one gain per frequency band** and applies it to the noisy spectrum. Bands follow a
Bark-like spacing, so the output dimension stays small and the model never has to learn what speech
sounds like — only which parts of the current frame are speech.

A gated recurrent unit carries the hidden state from frame to frame, which is what lets the model
tell a consonant apart from a transient: both are broadband and brief, but only one is consistent
with the last 200 ms of the signal. The hidden size is **128 units**, and a new set of gains is
produced every 10 ms.

### Quantisation-aware training

The model is trained with **int8 quantisation simulated in the loop**: weights and activations are
rounded to their deployment precision on every forward pass, while gradients still flow through in
float. The model therefore learns weights that survive being rounded, and the int8 model that ships
behaves like the float model that was evaluated.

<Why>
  Quantising after training is the cheaper route, and on a large model it costs
  almost nothing. On a model this small there is no redundancy to absorb the
  rounding error, and post-training quantisation shows up directly as audible
  artefacts in the gain mask. Simulating the quantiser during training removes
  that gap, at the price of a slower training run — which is the cheap resource
  here.
</Why>
