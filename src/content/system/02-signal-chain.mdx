Everything below happens once per **10 ms frame**. The frame length is the unit of the whole system:
the adaptive filter updates on it, the network's hidden state advances on it, and the latency budget
is counted in it.

1. **Capture.** The primary microphone sits on the headset boom, close to the mouth. The reference
   microphone sits on the enclosure, away from the speaker, so it hears the environment far more than
   the voice.
2. **DSP prefilter.** Beamforming gives a first spatial rejection; the NLMS adaptive filter then uses
   the reference channel to estimate and subtract what is left of the steady noise. See
   [section 04](#dsp-prefilter).
3. **SNR gate.** The prefiltered frame's SNR is estimated and compared against **+5 dB**. Above the
   threshold the frame is already clean enough and skips the network. Below it, the frame is handed
   to RNNoise.
4. **RNNoise.** The network predicts one gain per frequency band and applies it to the frame's
   spectrum. See [section 05](#rnnoise).
5. **Post-filter.** A comb filter tuned to the detected pitch removes residual noise between the
   harmonics. See [section 06](#post-filter).
6. **Output.** The frame is reconstructed, converted by the DAC and sent to the headset over the
   wireless link.

<Why>
  The gate exists because the two halves of the chain are good at different things. Above roughly
  +5 dB the residual noise is mostly stationary, and the DSP stage alone already scores better on
  perceptual metrics than the network does — while costing a fraction of the cycles. Below it, or as
  soon as impulsive events appear, the linear filter has nothing to track and the network is the only
  stage that can help. Gating rather than always running the network is what keeps the average
  per-frame budget inside a microcontroller.
</Why>
