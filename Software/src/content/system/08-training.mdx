### Loss

Training uses a perceptual loss over the band gains, combined with **L1** and **L2** terms on the
error between the predicted and the ideal gain. L2 keeps the bulk of the mask close to correct; L1
stops a handful of badly wrong bands from being averaged away; the perceptual weighting decides which
bands the error is worth spending capacity on.

<Why>
  These losses suit the failure mode this system cares about. An impulsive event
  is a small number of frames in which the mask must change sharply, and a pure
  mean-squared objective is happy to smear that error across neighbouring frames
  if it lowers the average. Adding the L1 term penalises the outlier directly.
  They are also arithmetically trivial, which keeps training fast enough to
  iterate on a model small enough to fit the MCU — the constraint every other
  choice here answers to.
</Why>

### Metrics

- **SNR** — how much louder the speech is than what is left of the noise, in dB. Blunt, but it is
  the number the rest of the chain is tuned against.
- **STOI** — short-time objective intelligibility, 0 to 1. Estimates how much of the speech a
  listener would actually make out. This is the number that matters for radio traffic.
- **PESQ** — perceptual evaluation of speech quality, roughly 1 to 4.5. Estimates how the result
  _sounds_: whether the processing introduced artefacts while it was removing noise.

All three are computed against the clean reference from the generated pairs, so they measure the
model, not the recording.
